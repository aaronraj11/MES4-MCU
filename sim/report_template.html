MES4-MCU · LTspice simulations

Schematic Rev A-d6 (14 Oct 2026) · simulated 29 Oct 2026 · Monkey Robotics · Aaron Raj Bhatti

Ten LTspice benches cover every analog and power circuit on the module, grouped by schematic sheet like the MES4-Carrier-EC benches, plus one bench that checks the U2 regulator model against TI's datasheet. The +5V benches are fed by the real MES4-Carrier-EC 5 V path (U1 URB2405S-6WR3, F2, FB1, C6–C9). No circuit fails. Three results changed a bring-up limit or a note (section 2, done 29 Oct).

2 · Findings and changes made

  • Buzzer current (BZ1). With the coil modelled as 25 Ω plus inductance, beeping adds 69–95 mA to the module's input current, depending on the coil inductance (0.1–1 mH, not published). The Power notes and BRINGUP step 12 say "≤ 80 mA". The real figure should be lower, because the buzzer's mechanical resonance raises its impedance at 4 kHz and the model leaves that out, but 80–95 mA is still possible. Done: BRINGUP step 12 pass limit is now "≤ 100 mA extra". Keep tones short: the coil dissipates 0.3–0.5 W while it beeps.
  • Negative transients on +5V (PWR3). When D2 conducts forward (+5V at −2.6 V at its 19.4 A rating), U2's body diode empties the 3V3 capacitors into it. 3V3 goes to −1.7 V for about 3 ms, while the STM32's absolute minimum is −0.3 V. This is MES4-CIF rule 4: reverse and surge protection is the carrier's job. The MES4-Carrier-EC's +5V comes from an isolated converter through F2 and FB1, so it can't do this. Done: MES4-CIF rule 4 and the rules box on sheet CAN_M2 now say to keep +5V above −0.3 V at the fingers.
  • NRST release time (MCU2). The MCU-sheet note says the pull-up "recharges C6 in ≈ 4 ms". The bench gives 4.9 ms to VIH with the typical 40 kΩ, and 3.0–6.7 ms over the 25–55 kΩ range. Done: the MCU-sheet note and MES4-CIF §4a now say "≈ 5 ms (3–7 ms)". There's no functional impact.
  • Hot-plug (PWR2). Nothing on the module is overstressed (+5V peaks at 5.09 V, so D2 stays off), but the contacts carry a 96 A spike for under a microsecond and the carrier socket dips to 2.3 V for about 5 µs. Rule 5 (no hot-plug) stands as written. That dip, plus the fingers mating in random order, is reason enough.

PWR1 · power-up (pwr1_powerup.net)

The carrier's U1 output ramps 0 → 5 V in 2 ms, or in 100 µs as a fast corner. U2 turns on when +5V passes its 2.2 V UVLO, and 3V3 reaches 3.0 V 0.45 ms later (500 µs soft-start). 3V3 doesn't overshoot (3.306 V max) and settles at 3.300 V. Peak input current is 0.16 A with the 2 ms ramp and 0.36 A with the 100 µs ramp, mostly C1 charging; U2's own inrush is held to 0.14 A by its soft-start. LED2 reaches 1 mA once +5V is above about 3.8 V and 3V3 above about 0.7 V (Q4's base), so it shows the rail is present, not that it's in regulation, as the notes say. Steady input current with these worst-case logic loads is 56 mA.

PWR2 · hot-plug, informative (pwr2_hotplug.net)

The module goes into a live slot: the carrier's C8 at the socket is at 5 V and the module's C1 is empty. The contact (10 mΩ, 3 nH, no bounce modelled) carries a 96 A spike, rung out within about 1 µs: the two ceramics share 24 µC through almost no impedance, so the gold fingers will spark. The socket voltage drops to 2.3 V and is back above 4.5 V within 4.5 µs. Module +5V peaks at 5.09 V, so D2 never reaches breakdown. The 20 mA it shows is its junction capacitance charging. U2 starts normally and 3V3 doesn't overshoot. Nothing here damages the module, but the spike and the dip land on everything else on the carrier's +5V. Back-powering through I/O fingers that mate before GND is not modelled. Rule 5 stays.

PWR3 · pulse at D2's rating (pwr3_tvs_pulse.net)

19.4 A, 10/1000 µs, injected straight at the fingers, positive at 1 ms and negative at 8.5 ms. The 5 V source here can't sink current, so D2 takes the whole positive pulse. Positive: +5V clamps at 10.3 V, D2 carries 19.4 A and 0.24 J. U2 (18 V abs), C1 (25 V) and C10 (16 V) are fine. 3V3 doesn't move (3.306 V max). Because the source can't sink, the pulse's long tail keeps D2 clamping, and +5V sits near 7–8 V until 8.5 ms. PB2 peaks at 2.76 V: C9 filters out the brief 10.3 V peak, leaving 8.3 V / 3. That's below the 3.43 V worst case in the notes, and PB2 is FT_ea anyway. Negative: D2 conducts forward and +5V reaches −2.6 V. U2's body diode then discharges the 3V3 capacitors into the input, with up to 8.3 A flowing back through U2 in this model. 3V3 stays below −0.3 V from 8.5 to 11.4 ms and is back above 3.2 V at 12.1 ms. The MCU, X1 and U3 would be reverse-biased. See section 2: this is the carrier's job.

PWR5 · 3V3 load steps (pwr5_3v3_load.net)

From 1.0 to 1.5 ms, CAN traffic at the bit-stuffing worst case (5 dominant / 1 recessive): U3 draws its 17 mA maximum plus 50 mA into the bus while dominant. At 2 ms the carrier adds 50 mA on pin 4 in 1 µs, and at 3 ms both loads switch off. 3V3 stays within 3.293–3.307 V (±7 mV) throughout, and module +5V dips to 4.973 V at most. The U2 model is fitted to a 1 µF output, so the module's ≈ 14 µF (C2 + C5 + 4 × 100 nF) makes these figures conservative.

Parts: X1 SX2M 8 MHz XO → R14 22 Ω → PC14 (HSE bypass), routed lengths from the PCB (HSE_XO 2.29 mm, HSE_IN 2.04 mm, F.Cu over In1 GND, no vias) · NRST: STM32 pull-up RPU 25–55 kΩ, C6 100 nF, 30 mm to the fingers.

MCU1 · HSE clock edge (mcu1_hse_clock.net)

X1's edge rate and output resistance aren't published, so this uses a fast corner: 1.5 ns edges from 15 Ω. The 4.3 mm of trace is far shorter than an edge, so it is lumped with the X1 and LQFP-32 lead inductance (5.2 nH total) into PC14's 5.6 pF. Without R14, PC14 rings to 3.55 V and −0.25 V. With R14 = 22 Ω, it peaks at 3.37 V and −0.07 V, about 3.5× less ringing, and the 20–80 % edge slows by only 0.03 ns (0.84 → 0.87 ns). Neither case forward-biases PC14's clamp diodes (injection under 1 nA), and the duty cycle at VDD/2 stays 50.0 %, inside the PLL's 45–55 %. R14 does its job.

MCU2 · NRST (mcu2_nrst.net)

3V3 rises as in PWR1. The STM32 holds NRST low until VDD passes about 2.0 V (POR / BOR), then RPU charges C6. NRST reaches VIH (0.7 × VDD) 3.0 / 4.9 / 6.7 ms after POR for RPU 25 / 40 / 55 kΩ. A 20 µs internal reset pulse (driver assumed 50 Ω) pulls C6 down to 53–65 mV, well below VIL (0.99 V), so ST's 20 µs pulse still reaches the pin through 100 nF. Recovery takes 3.0–6.5 ms. A 1 ms button press takes NRST to ≈ 0 V, with 3.0–6.6 ms recovery.

CAN2 · series resistors on CANH / CANL (can2_series_r.net)

MES4-CIF rule 8 says "no series resistors on CAN". Here a carrier adds Rs in each line between the fingers and a bus terminated at both ends (60 Ω):

22 Ω already falls below the 1.5 V that ISO 11898-2 asks of a transmitter. From 47 Ω, receivers with a 0.9 V threshold can miss dominant bits. Rule 8 is right as written: TVS only, no resistors.

6 · Not simulated, and why

  • I²C1: the pull-ups are on the carrier. MES4-Carrier-EC MAIN1 covers 400 kHz with 2.2 kΩ and 60 pF (111 ns rise).
  • ESD on CANH / CANL and the fingers: how the current splits between D1 and U3 depends on U3's internal clamp, which TI doesn't publish. Datasheet ratings: NUP2105L IEC 61000-4-2 ±30 kV contact; SN65HVD230 bus pins ±16 kV HBM.
  • CAN shorted to 24 V: above U3's 16 V absolute maximum, so destroyed by datasheet (MES4-CIF rule 9). Nothing to simulate.
  • Pin 4 (3V3) shorted: U2 folds back to 150–350 mA (DRV package), so the module draws ≤ ≈ 0.37 A from +5V. U2 then dissipates up to 1.8 W and cycles in thermal shutdown (180 °C). That's thermal behaviour, not modelled.
  • SWD, VCP, NRST and GPIO fingers: short, low-speed, carrier-specific lines. USB D+/D− are unused.
  • STM32 internals: PLL lock, ADC sampling and the exact POR / BOR timing are taken from ST's data, not simulated.

7 · Models and assumptions

  • LTspice 17.2.4 (macOS). Benches and shared models are in MES4-MCU/sim/: models.inc (device models), module.inc (module power network) and carrier5v.inc (MES4-Carrier-EC +5V). Run python3 run_sims.py, then python3 make_report.py.
  • U2 TLV76733: a behavioural model fitted to TI SLVSE84D. UVLO 2.2 V / 130 mV hysteresis, 500 µs soft-start, current limit 1.3 A folding back to 0.25 A, dropout 0.9 Ω, no sinking, PMOS body diode OUT → IN, −60 dB supply ripple above 1 kHz. The control loop (PI, 6 A/V, plus a 0.15 µs pole) is tuned to TI Fig. 7-12. MDL1 below checks the fit: −109 / +105 mV against TI's ≈ −120 / +100 mV. Steps from a near-zero load are worse in silicon (TI Fig. 7-11: about −400 mV from 1 mA), but the module never draws less than about 30 mA from 3V3.
  • U3 SN65HVD230: bus-side behavioural model. Dominant CANH from VCC − 0.1 V and CANL to 0.75 V through Rd/2 each, with Rd = 13.5 Ω (typical, VOD 2.0 V) or 38 Ω (minimum, 1.5 V). VCC current is 10 mA plus the bus current. Driver and receiver propagation delays are not modelled.
  • D2 SMF6.0A: VBR 7.0 V at 10 mA, Rs 0.16 Ω (10.3 V at 19.4 A), 1.5 nF. D1 NUP2105L: two anti-series 29 V junctions per line, 0.5 Ω, 26 pF.
  • Q3 AO3400A and D3 1N4148WS: the same models as the MES4-Carrier-EC benches. Q1 / Q2 / Q4 MMBT3904: 2N3904 parameters. LEDs: fitted to the forward voltages in the Power notes (red / yellow 2.0 V at 3 mA, green / white 2.8 V at 2 mA). The LED currents therefore check the transistor stages and the supply range, not the LEDs themselves.
  • Ceramic capacitors at their DC-bias value: 10 µF 25 V 0805 = 5 µF at 5 V and 6.9 µF at 3.3 V (Power notes). 100 nF parts with 20 mΩ ESR.
  • STM32G0B1: 20 mA at 64 MHz once VDD > 1.6 V; GPIO output 40 Ω; PC14 5 pF plus a 3 nH lead, clamp diodes to the rails; NRST pull-up 25 / 40 / 55 kΩ, POR / BOR release at about 2.0 V, internal reset driver 50 Ω (assumed).
  • X1 SX2M: 1.5 ns edges from 15 Ω (fast corner; the datasheet gives neither). BZ1 KLJ-5018-5040: 25 Ω coil with inductance stepped 0.1–1 mH; mechanical resonance not modelled.
  • Carrier +5V: the MES4-Carrier-EC network from its bench PWR5. The URB2405S-6WR3 start-up ramp isn't published; 2 ms and 100 µs are used. Benches with a fixed supply ramp it from 0 V over the first 20 µs so LTspice starts from rest.
  • CAN cable: two ideal 60 Ω lines at 5 ns/m (120 Ω differential). Common-mode impedance and cable loss are not modelled.

MDL1 · U2 model check (mdl1_tlv767_fit.net)

The same test as TI Fig. 7-12: VIN 5 V, COUT 1 µF, 250 → 850 mA in 0.75 µs, then back. The model dips to 3.190 V (−109 mV) and overshoots to 3.405 V (+105 mV), against TI's ≈ −120 / +100 mV. It recovers more slowly than the silicon: about 40 µs, against roughly 10 µs in TI's plot. That makes the PWR5 and BZ1 3V3 figures pessimistic, not optimistic.
